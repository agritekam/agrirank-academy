    • 14. Nutrient Management

    Tomato is a nutrient-responsive crop. Balanced nutrition is essential for vegetative growth, flowering, fruit setting, fruit development and quality.

    Major Nutrients

    • Nitrogen: promotes vegetative growth but excessive nitrogen may delay maturity and favour excessive foliage.
    • Phosphorus: important for root development and reproductive growth.
    • Potassium: important for fruit development, quality and plant physiological processes.

    Secondary and Micronutrients

    • Calcium is important for cell-wall development and fruit quality.
    • Magnesium is a component of chlorophyll.
    • Boron and other micronutrients are required in small quantities.
    • Deficiencies should be diagnosed before corrective application.
    Best Practice: Use soil testing, crop requirement, recommended regional fertilizer schedule and split application/fertigation where appropriate rather than applying nutrients blindly.

    15. Intercultural Operations

    • Weed control during early crop growth is important.
    • Mulching reduces weed emergence and conserves moisture.
    • Earthing-up may be useful in appropriate production systems.
    • Removal of severely diseased plant parts helps sanitation.
    • Staking and training improve canopy management in indeterminate plants.

    16. Staking, Training & Pruning

    Staking

    Plants are supported with stakes, strings or trellises to keep foliage and fruit away from the ground and improve light penetration and air movement.

    Training

    Training directs plant growth along the support system. It is especially important in indeterminate tomato under protected or intensive systems.

    Pruning

    Selective removal of unwanted shoots or leaves may be practiced depending on the production system. Excessive pruning should be avoided because healthy leaves are required for photosynthesis.

    17. Flowering, Pollination & Fruit Set

    • Tomato flowers are bisexual.
    • Self-pollination is predominant.
    • Temperature, humidity and pollen viability influence fruit set.
    • Extreme heat can cause poor pollen viability and flower drop.
    • Good plant nutrition and water management support fruit setting.
    • In protected cultivation, mechanical or assisted pollination may be used where required.

    18. Physiological Disorders

    Blossom End Rot

    A dark, sunken lesion develops near the blossom end of the fruit. It is associated with inadequate calcium supply to rapidly developing fruit and is often aggravated by irregular water availability.

    Management: Maintain uniform soil moisture, avoid severe water stress, maintain balanced nutrition and correct calcium availability based on diagnosis.

    Fruit Cracking

    Cracks develop on the fruit surface, often following rapid fruit growth after fluctuations in moisture availability.

    Management: Maintain uniform irrigation, avoid severe moisture fluctuation, use suitable varieties and maintain balanced nutrition.

    Puffiness

    Fruits may have poorly developed locules and appear light or hollow. It may be associated with poor fertilization and environmental or nutritional conditions.

    Sunscald

    Exposed portions of fruit may develop pale, yellow or damaged areas due to excessive solar radiation and high temperature.

    Management: Maintain adequate foliage cover and avoid unnecessary removal of healthy leaves.

    Blotchy Ripening

    Uneven colour development occurs on fruit. Environmental conditions, nutrition and cultivar characteristics can influence the disorder.

    19. Weed Management

    • Maintain a weed-free crop during early growth.
    • Hand weeding and intercultivation can be used where suitable.
    • Mulching suppresses weed emergence.
    • Herbicides may be used only according to crop-specific label recommendations.
    • Integrated weed management is preferred over dependence on a single method.

    20. Major Insect Pests of Tomato & Complete Management

    20.1 Tomato Fruit Borer – Helicoverpa armigera

    Identification

    Larvae are greenish, brownish or variable in colour and may have longitudinal stripes. Adults are medium-sized moths.

    Damage Symptoms

    • Larvae feed on leaves and flowers.
    • They bore into developing fruits.
    • Round holes are visible on fruits.
    • Frass is often present around the bore hole.
    • Damaged fruits may develop secondary rotting.

    Monitoring

    • Regularly inspect flowers and fruits.
    • Use pheromone traps for monitoring adult moth activity.
    • Inspect fruits for fresh bore holes and larval presence.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Hand-pick larvae whenever practical.
    • Collect and destroy bored fruits.
    • Use pheromone traps for monitoring and mass-trapping support.
    • Light traps may be used as a supplementary monitoring tool.

    Biological Management

    • Trichogramma chilonis, T. pretiosum and other suitable egg parasitoids may be used where recommended.
    • HaNPV can be incorporated into an IPM programme.
    • Conserve predatory insects and other natural enemies.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem-based products containing azadirachtin may be used according to label recommendations.

    Chemical Management

    • Emamectin benzoate is one of the active ingredients used against important lepidopteran pests where registered and recommended.
    • Other registered selective insecticides may be used according to the current label.
    • Rotate insecticides belonging to different IRAC mode-of-action groups.
    IPM: Monitoring + pheromone traps + destruction of infested fruits + natural enemies + parasitoids/HaNPV + neem-based products + need-based selective insecticide.

    20.2 Whitefly – Bemisia tabaci

    Damage Symptoms

    • Nymphs and adults suck plant sap.
    • Leaves become yellow and weak under heavy infestation.
    • Honeydew promotes sooty mould.
    • Whitefly is an important vector of tomato leaf curl viruses.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Install yellow sticky traps for monitoring and suppression.
    • Use insect-proof nursery structures where suitable.
    • Remove severely infested plants when appropriate.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve parasitoids such as Encarsia and predators.
    • Entomopathogenic fungi such as Beauveria bassiana may be used where recommended.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem oil may be used as a botanical component of IPM according to label recommendations.
    • Azadirachtin-based formulations can be used where registered.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered whitefly insecticides only when necessary.
    • Active ingredients such as flonicamid or other registered selective products may be used according to current recommendations.
    • Rotate modes of action to reduce resistance development.
    IPM: Healthy seedlings + yellow sticky traps + weed management + natural enemies + neem/azadirachtin + need-based selective insecticide.

    20.3 Thrips – Thrips tabaci / Frankliniella schultzei

    Damage

    • Nymphs and adults scrape and suck plant tissues.
    • Leaves may show silvery patches and distortion.
    • Flowers may be damaged.
    • Fruit setting may be reduced under severe infestation.
    • Some thrips species are important vectors of plant viruses.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Use blue or yellow sticky traps according to the monitoring programme.
    • Remove heavily infested plant parts where practical.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve predatory mites and predatory insects.
    • Beauveria bassiana and other microbial products may be used where recommended.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem oil or azadirachtin-based products may be used according to label recommendations.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered selective insecticides when necessary.
    • Rotate insecticides according to their mode of action.

    20.4 Aphids – Aphis gossypii / Myzus persicae

    Damage

    • Aphids suck sap from tender shoots and leaves.
    • Leaves may curl and become distorted.
    • Honeydew promotes sooty mould.
    • Aphids may transmit plant viruses.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Remove heavily infested shoots where practical.
    • Use sticky traps for monitoring winged aphids.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve ladybird beetles.
    • Conserve lacewings.
    • Conserve aphid parasitoids.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem oil/azadirachtin-based products may be used according to label recommendations.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered selective aphicides only when necessary.
    • Flonicamid and other registered active ingredients may be used according to current label recommendations.

    20.5 Tobacco Caterpillar – Spodoptera litura

    Damage

    • Young larvae scrape leaf tissues.
    • Older larvae consume large portions of leaves.
    • Severe infestation causes defoliation.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Collect egg masses and larvae manually.
    • Use pheromone traps for monitoring.
    • Destroy heavily infested leaves when practical.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve parasitoids and predators.
    • Use Beauveria bassiana or other suitable microbial products where recommended.
    • Use suitable viral biopesticides where recommended.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem/azadirachtin formulations may be incorporated into IPM.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered selective insecticides when required.
    • Emamectin benzoate is an example of an active ingredient used against important caterpillar pests where registered.
    • Rotate modes of action.

    20.6 Serpentine Leaf Miner – Liriomyza trifolii

    Damage

    • Larvae make serpentine mines inside leaves.
    • Photosynthetic area is reduced.
    • Severe infestation causes premature leaf drying.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Remove and destroy severely mined leaves.
    • Use yellow sticky traps for adult monitoring.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve parasitoids attacking leaf miner larvae.
    • Avoid unnecessary broad-spectrum insecticides that destroy parasitoids.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem-based products may be used as part of an integrated programme.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered selective insecticides only when necessary.
    • Rotate modes of action to reduce resistance.

    20.7 Tomato Pinworm – Tuta absoluta

    Importance

    Tuta absoluta is an invasive tomato pest and can damage leaves, shoots and fruits.

    Damage

    • Larvae make mines in leaves.
    • Larvae attack shoots and growing points.
    • They bore into fruits.
    • Damaged fruits may develop secondary infections.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Use pheromone traps for monitoring.
    • Remove and destroy infested leaves and fruits.
    • Destroy crop residues after harvest.
    • Use insect-proof nursery structures where suitable.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve parasitoids and predators.
    • Use suitable microbial biopesticides where recommended.

    Botanical Management

    • Azadirachtin/neem-based products may be used as part of IPM where registered.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered insecticides according to current recommendations.
    • Rotate insecticides with different modes of action.
    • Avoid repeated use of the same active ingredient.
    Important: Tuta absoluta requires strict monitoring and integrated management because resistance can develop quickly.

    20.8 Cutworm – Agrotis spp.

    Damage

    • Larvae cut young seedlings near the soil surface.
    • Severe attack results in gaps in plant population.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Hand collection of larvae at night can be practiced where feasible.
    • Deep ploughing can expose soil stages to predators and environmental conditions.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve natural enemies.
    • Use suitable entomopathogenic products where recommended.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered treatment only when necessary and according to label recommendations.

    20.9 Hadda Beetle / Epilachna Beetle – Epilachna spp.

    Damage

    • Adults and grubs feed on leaves.
    • Leaf tissue may become skeletonized.
    • Severe infestation reduces photosynthetic area.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Hand-pick adults, larvae and egg masses where practical.
    • Remove heavily infested leaves.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve predators and parasitoids.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem-based products may be used where recommended.

    Chemical Management

    • Use registered insecticides only when necessary.

    21. Major Mite Pest

    21.1 Red Spider Mite – Tetranychus spp.

    Mites are arachnids and are therefore discussed separately from insect pests.

    Damage

    • Mites suck sap mainly from the lower surface of leaves.
    • Leaves develop yellow speckling.
    • Leaves may become bronzed and dry.
    • Severe infestation causes premature defoliation.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Remove severely infested leaves.
    • Maintain adequate irrigation and avoid plant stress.
    • Wash foliage with suitable water pressure where practical.

    Biological Management

    • Conserve predatory mites.
    • Conserve predatory insects.

    Botanical Management

    • Neem oil or neem soap formulations may be used according to label recommendations.

    Chemical Management

    • Registered acaricides may include active ingredients such as propargite or spiromesifen where approved for the crop and target pest.
    • Rotate acaricides according to mode of action.

    22. Fungal & Oomycete Diseases

    22.1 Damping-off

    Causal Organisms

    Common nursery damping-off organisms include Pythium spp. and other soil-borne pathogens depending on the region and nursery conditions.

    Symptoms

    • Seeds may rot before emergence.
    • Post-emergence seedlings develop water-soaked lesions near the soil line.
    • Seedlings collapse and die.

    Management

    • Use clean nursery media.
    • Use disease-free seed.
    • Avoid excessive irrigation.
    • Maintain proper drainage.
    • Use Trichoderma-based biological products where recommended.
    • Use registered fungicidal seed/nursery treatment according to label recommendations.

    22.2 Early Blight – Alternaria solani

    Symptoms

    • Dark brown lesions develop on older leaves.
    • Concentric rings may form inside lesions.
    • Severe infection causes defoliation.
    • Stem and fruit infection can also occur.

    Management

    • Use healthy planting material.
    • Remove infected crop residues.
    • Follow crop rotation.
    • Maintain proper spacing and canopy aeration.
    • Avoid prolonged leaf wetness.
    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
    • Use registered fungicides such as copper-based products, mancozeb or other recommended fungicides according to the current label.
    • Rotate fungicide modes of action.

    22.3 Late Blight – Phytophthora infestans

    Symptoms

    • Water-soaked irregular lesions develop on leaves.
    • Lesions rapidly enlarge under cool and humid conditions.
    • White sporulation may appear on the lower surface during favourable conditions.
    • Stem and fruit infection may occur.

    Management

    • Remove infected plant material.
    • Improve field aeration.
    • Avoid unnecessary overhead irrigation.
    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
    • Use registered fungicides such as copper-based products or appropriate oomycete fungicides according to local advisory.
    • Rotate fungicide modes of action.

    22.4 Septoria Leaf Spot – Septoria lycopersici

    Symptoms

    • Small circular leaf spots develop.
    • Spots may have dark margins and greyish centres.
    • Black fruiting structures may occur inside lesions.
    • Severe infection results in defoliation.

    Management

    • Remove infected plant debris.
    • Use healthy seed.
    • Maintain sanitation.
    • Avoid overhead irrigation.
    • Use registered fungicides according to current recommendations.

    22.5 Fusarium Wilt – Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici

    Symptoms

    • Lower leaves become yellow.
    • Plants gradually wilt.
    • Vascular browning can be observed inside the stem.
    • Plants may become stunted.

    Management

    • Use resistant varieties where available.
    • Use healthy seedlings.
    • Remove severely infected plants.
    • Follow crop rotation.
    • Use biological soil management such as suitable Trichoderma formulations where recommended.
    • Maintain nursery sanitation.

    22.6 Verticillium Wilt – Verticillium spp.

    Symptoms

    • Lower leaves become yellow.
    • Wilting and stunting occur.
    • Vascular discoloration may be present.

    Management

    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
    • Use healthy seedlings.
    • Follow suitable crop rotation.
    • Maintain sanitation.

    22.7 Powdery Mildew

    Symptoms

    • White powdery growth develops on leaves.
    • Leaves may yellow and dry under severe infection.

    Management

    • Improve canopy ventilation.
    • Remove severely infected leaves.
    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
    • Use registered fungicides according to label recommendations.

    23. Bacterial Diseases

    23.1 Bacterial Wilt – Ralstonia solanacearum

    Symptoms

    • Plants show sudden or progressive wilting.
    • Leaves may initially remain green.
    • Vascular tissue becomes discoloured.
    • Warm and wet soil conditions favour disease development.

    Management

    • Use bacterial-wilt-resistant varieties/hybrids where available.
    • Use healthy seedlings.
    • Remove infected plants.
    • Follow crop rotation.
    • Avoid movement of contaminated soil and irrigation water.
    • Use biological soil management where recommended.
    • Maintain strict field sanitation.

    23.2 Bacterial Canker – Clavibacter michiganensis

    Symptoms

    • Leaf margins may scorch.
    • Plants may wilt.
    • Stem cankers may develop.
    • Fruit may develop small lesions or spots.

    Mechanical / Physical Management

    • Remove infected plants.
    • Disinfect pruning and cutting tools.
    • Avoid unnecessary handling of wet plants.

    Management

    • Use certified disease-free seed.
    • Use clean nursery material.
    • Maintain sanitation.
    • Use resistant/tolerant material where available.

    23.3 Bacterial Spot – Xanthomonas spp.

    Symptoms

    • Small dark water-soaked spots develop on leaves.
    • Spots may enlarge and cause yellowing.
    • Fruit lesions may become raised and scabby.

    Management

    • Use disease-free seed.
    • Maintain nursery sanitation.
    • Avoid overhead irrigation.
    • Remove infected residues.
    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
    • Use registered copper-based bactericidal products where recommended.

    23.4 Bacterial Speck – Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato

    Symptoms

    • Small dark lesions occur on leaves.
    • Small black spots may develop on fruits.
    • Cool and wet conditions may favour disease development.

    Management

    • Use healthy seed.
    • Maintain nursery sanitation.
    • Avoid working in wet foliage.
    • Remove infected crop residues.
    • Use resistant varieties where available.

    24. Viral Diseases

    24.1 Tomato Leaf Curl Disease

    Symptoms

    • Leaves curl and become distorted.
    • Leaves may become smaller and yellowish.
    • Plants become severely stunted.
    • Flowering and fruit set are reduced.

    Important Vector

    Whitefly, particularly Bemisia tabaci, is an important vector of tomato leaf curl virus complexes.

    Management

    • Use resistant/tolerant varieties or hybrids where available.
    • Raise healthy seedlings under protected nursery conditions.
    • Remove virus-infected plants as recommended.
    • Control whitefly through IPM.
    • Remove weeds and alternate hosts.
    • Use yellow sticky traps.

    24.2 Tomato Mosaic Disease

    Symptoms

    • Mosaic pattern develops on leaves.
    • Leaves may become distorted.
    • Plants may become stunted.
    • Fruit size and quality may decline.
