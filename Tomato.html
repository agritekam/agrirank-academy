🍅 Tomato

Complete ICAR PG / JRF Vegetable Science Notes

🌱 1. Introduction

Tomato is one of the most important vegetable crops grown throughout the world. It is cultivated for fresh consumption as well as for processing into products such as juice, puree, ketchup and paste.

Scientific name: Solanum lycopersicum L.
Family: Solanaceae
Chromosome number: 2n = 24

🌍 2. Origin & Centre of Origin

  • Tomato originated in the Andean region of South America.
  • Peru and Ecuador are associated with its centre of origin.
  • Mexico played an important role in its domestication and spread.

📚 3. Classification

  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Genus: Solanum
  • Species: Solanum lycopersicum
  • Tomato is generally treated as a self-pollinated crop, although some natural cross-pollination can occur.

🌿 4. Botanical Description

  • Herbaceous plant, generally grown as an annual crop.
  • Stem becomes branched and may require support in indeterminate types.
  • Leaves are compound and aromatic.
  • Flowers are generally yellow and borne in clusters.
  • Fruit is a berry.
  • Fruit colour changes from green to red or other colours depending on cultivar.

🌱 5. Important Varieties / Types

  • Determinate types
  • Indeterminate types
  • Semi-determinate types
  • Fresh-market cultivars
  • Processing cultivars
  • Hybrid tomatoes
  • Cherry tomato types

Variety and hybrid selection should be based on season, production system, market requirement and resistance to important diseases.

🌦️ 7. Climate

  • Tomato performs well under moderate climatic conditions.
  • Very high temperature can adversely affect flowering and fruit set.
  • Very low temperature can also interfere with normal growth and fruit development.
  • Good sunlight is important for healthy growth and fruit quality.

🌱 8. Soil

  • Well-drained fertile soil is preferred.
  • Loam to sandy-loam soils are commonly suitable.
  • Good organic matter improves soil condition and crop growth.
  • Waterlogging should be avoided.

🌱 9. Nursery Raising

  • Healthy and disease-free seed should be selected.
  • Nursery beds or suitable nursery structures should have good drainage.
  • Seedlings should receive proper moisture and nutrition.
  • Hardening of seedlings before transplanting helps establish the crop.
  • Healthy seedlings are preferred for field planting.

🌿 10. Transplanting & Spacing

  • Transplanting should be done when seedlings are healthy and sufficiently developed.
  • Spacing depends on variety, plant type, season and production system.
  • Indeterminate varieties often require staking or trellising.
  • Proper spacing improves aeration and light penetration.

💧 11. Irrigation & Nutrient Management

  • Regular and uniform moisture is important.
  • Excess irrigation may cause waterlogging and favour disease development.
  • Balanced application of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium is important.
  • Micronutrients should be supplied when deficiency is identified.
  • Fertigation can be used under suitable production systems.

🌸 12. Flowering & Fruit Set

  • Temperature strongly influences flowering and fruit set.
  • Extreme temperatures can cause poor fruit set.
  • Balanced nutrition and adequate moisture support fruit development.
  • Pollination conditions are important for good fruit set.

⚠️ 13. Physiological Disorders

🍅 Blossom End Rot

A dark, sunken lesion develops near the blossom end of the fruit. It is commonly associated with inadequate calcium availability in rapidly growing fruits, often aggravated by irregular moisture supply.

🍅 Fruit Cracking

Cracks develop on fruits, particularly when growth is rapid and moisture conditions fluctuate.

🍅 Puffiness

Fruits may develop poorly filled locules and become light in weight.

🍅 Sunscald

Exposed fruit surfaces can develop pale or damaged areas under excessive solar radiation and high temperature.

🐛 14. Major Insect Pests & Management

Tomato Fruit Borer

  • Larvae bore into fruits and feed internally.
  • Damaged fruits become unmarketable.
  • Remove and destroy damaged fruits and infested plant parts.
  • Use suitable biological control measures where appropriate.
  • Use need-based, label-recommended insecticides as part of IPM.

Whitefly

  • Adults and nymphs suck plant sap.
  • Honeydew and sooty mould may develop.
  • Whiteflies can also transmit important viral diseases.
  • Use clean planting material and monitor the crop regularly.
  • Use biological and need-based chemical control within an IPM programme.

Thrips

  • Cause feeding damage on young leaves and plant parts.
  • May contribute to transmission of some viral diseases.
  • Regular monitoring and integrated management are important.

Cutworm

  • Young seedlings may be cut near the soil surface.
  • Field sanitation and removal of weeds help reduce infestation.
  • Need-based control can be used according to local recommendations.

🦠 15. Major Diseases & Management

Early Blight

  • Dark lesions develop on leaves and may show concentric rings.
  • Remove infected plant debris.
  • Maintain proper spacing and field sanitation.
  • Use disease-free seed/seedlings.
  • Apply recommended fungicides when required.

Late Blight

  • Water-soaked to dark lesions may develop on leaves and other plant parts.
  • Cool and humid conditions favour disease development.
  • Improve air circulation and avoid prolonged leaf wetness.
  • Use recommended fungicides according to label and local advisories.

Fusarium Wilt

  • Plants show wilting and yellowing.
  • Use healthy planting material and resistant varieties where available.
  • Crop rotation and sanitation are important.
  • Integrated soil and disease management should be followed.

Bacterial Wilt

  • Sudden wilting can occur while leaves may initially remain green.
  • Use disease-free planting material.
  • Remove infected plants and maintain field sanitation.
  • Use resistant/tolerant varieties where available.
  • Avoid movement of contaminated soil and irrigation water.

Tomato Leaf Curl Virus

  • Leaves may curl, become smaller and show yellowing.
  • Whitefly is an important vector.
  • Use healthy seedlings and resistant varieties where available.
  • Remove infected plants and manage vector populations through IPM.
  • Maintain weed and field sanitation.

🌾 16. Harvesting & Yield

  • Harvest stage depends on market distance and intended use.
  • For nearby markets, fruits may be harvested at a more advanced maturity stage.
  • For distant markets, fruits may be harvested at an appropriate earlier maturity stage.
  • Yield varies with variety, season, management and production system.

📦 17. Post-Harvest Management

  • Harvest fruits carefully to avoid mechanical injury.
  • Sort and grade according to size, colour and quality.
  • Remove damaged and diseased fruits.
  • Use suitable packaging and storage conditions.
  • Minimise bruising during transportation.

🎯 18. ICAR PG Important Points

  • Scientific name: Solanum lycopersicum
  • Family: Solanaceae
  • Chromosome number: 2n = 24
  • Fruit type: Berry
  • Major physiological disorder: Blossom end rot
  • Important insect pest: Tomato fruit borer
  • Important viral disease: Tomato leaf curl
  • Indeterminate types generally require support under suitable production systems.

B. 2n = 20

Answer: C. 2n = 24
Q3. Tomato fruit is botanically a:

A. Drupe
B. Berry
C. Capsule
D. Pod

Answer: B. Berry
Q4. Blossom end rot is associated mainly with:

A. Calcium-related disorder
B. Iron toxicity
C. Nitrogen fixation
D. Pollination only

Answer: A. Calcium-related disorder
Q5. Tomato fruit borer damages mainly:

A. Roots
B. Fruits
C. Seeds only
D. Flowers only

Answer: B. Fruits
