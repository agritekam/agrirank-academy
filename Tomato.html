🍅 Tomato

Complete Crop Notes | AgriRank Academy

1. Introduction

Tomato is one of the most important vegetable crops of the world. It is widely cultivated for fresh consumption as well as for processing into products such as puree, paste, ketchup, sauce and juice.

Tomato belongs to the family Solanaceae and is an important warm-season vegetable crop. The crop has high economic importance because of its nutritional value, wide adaptability and large processing industry.

🌍 Origin & Distribution

Tomato originated in the western region of South America and was subsequently spread to different parts of the world. Today it is cultivated widely in tropical, subtropical and temperate regions.

🥗 Nutritional & Economic Importance

  • Tomato is an important source of vitamins, minerals and antioxidants.
  • It is widely consumed as a fresh vegetable.
  • It is an important raw material for processing industries.
  • Tomato products include puree, paste, ketchup, sauce and juice.
  • The crop provides employment and income to farmers and allied industries.

2. Plant Description

Tomato is a herbaceous plant with a highly branched shoot system, well-developed root system, compound leaves, yellow flowers and fleshy berry fruits. Its growth habit and branching pattern vary with genotype and cultivation system.

Complete tomato plant showing major vegetative and reproductive parts.

🔍 Identify the Plant: Root → Stem → Leaf → Flower → Fruit

🌱 2.1 Root System

Tomato develops a well-branched root system. When plants are raised from seed and transplanted, the original primary root may be disturbed and a large number of lateral and adventitious roots can develop. The root system is important for anchorage, absorption of water and mineral nutrients, and storage of assimilates.

Important Features

  • Root system is extensively branched.
  • Primary and lateral roots form a network in the soil.
  • Adventitious roots can develop from the stem, especially when stem portions are in contact with moist soil.
  • Good root development is important for vigorous vegetative growth and fruit production.
  • Root development is strongly influenced by soil moisture, aeration, temperature and soil structure.

Major Root Regions

  • Primary root: The main root developing from the radicle.
  • Lateral roots: Branches arising from the main and other roots.
  • Root hairs: Fine structures that increase the effective absorbing surface.
  • Root tip: Actively growing region involved in root extension.

Tomato root architecture and root development reference.

🎯 PG/JRF Point: Tomato can develop adventitious roots from stem portions under suitable moist conditions. This is one reason why proper transplanting and appropriate planting depth can influence root establishment.

🌿 2.2 Stem

The tomato stem is herbaceous and becomes more complex as the plant develops. It bears leaves, lateral branches, inflorescences and fruits. The stem contains vascular tissues responsible for transport of water, minerals and photosynthates.

Stem Characteristics

  • Young stems are green and herbaceous.
  • The stem bears nodes and internodes.
  • Leaves arise at nodes.
  • Axillary buds occur in the leaf axils.
  • Branches develop from lateral/axillary buds.
  • Trichomes are present on the stem surface.
  • Older portions may undergo secondary growth.

Growth Habit

Tomato shows a characteristic branching pattern in which the shoot changes from initial monopodial growth to a sympodial pattern after flowering begins. Cultivars also differ in their overall growth habit.

  • Determinate: Vegetative growth terminates after a limited number of flowering/branching units and the plant remains relatively compact.
  • Indeterminate: Vegetative growth continues for a longer period and the plant continues producing leaves and flowering branches.
  • Semi-determinate: Intermediate growth behaviour.

Node, Internode & Axillary Bud

  • Node: Point on the stem where leaves and axillary buds arise.
  • Internode: Stem portion between two successive nodes.
  • Axillary bud: Bud present in the axil of a leaf that can develop into a lateral shoot.

Schematic showing major plant structural regions including stem, nodes and roots.

🎯 Remember: Tomato's later shoot development shows a sympodial branching pattern. Determinate and indeterminate growth habits are important concepts in tomato production and training.

🍃 2.3 Leaf

Tomato has compound leaves. The leaf consists of a petiole and a rachis bearing leaflets. Tomato foliage is an important photosynthetic organ and contributes directly to carbohydrate production required for vegetative growth and fruit development.

Leaf Structure

  • Petiole: Connects the leaf to the stem.
  • Rachis: Central axis bearing the leaflets.
  • Leaflets: Individual units forming the compound leaf.
  • Midrib and veins: Provide vascular support and transport.
  • Trichomes: Hair-like structures present on the leaf surface.

Important Features

  • Leaves are compound.
  • Leaf arrangement on the stem is spiral.
  • Leaflets may themselves show compound characteristics.
  • Stomata regulate gaseous exchange and transpiration.
  • Trichomes are abundant on tomato foliage.

🎯 PG/JRF Point: Tomato leaf is compound and contains leaflets arranged along a rachis. The leaf also contains stomata, vascular tissues and trichomes.

🌼 2.4 Inflorescence

Tomato flowers are produced in inflorescences. Depending on genotype and environmental conditions, the inflorescence may be simple or more complex/branched. The number and arrangement of flowers influence flowering and subsequent fruit set.

Important Features

  • Inflorescences arise from the shoot system.
  • Flowers are usually yellow.
  • Simple and branched inflorescence forms can occur.
  • Flower number can vary with genotype and environmental conditions.
  • A single inflorescence can contain flower buds, open flowers and developing fruits at different stages.

Tomato flowers and developing fruits on an inflorescence.

🔍 Observe: In tomato, an inflorescence may simultaneously show buds, open flowers and developing fruits because flowers within the same cluster do not necessarily develop at exactly the same time.

🌼 2.5 Flower — Detailed Structure

The tomato flower is a bisexual flower containing both male and female reproductive organs. It has the typical floral whorls and is adapted primarily for self-pollination.

Main Floral Parts

  • Pedicel: Stalk supporting the individual flower.
  • Calyx: Consists of sepals and protects the developing bud.
  • Corolla: Consists of yellow petals.
  • Androecium: Male reproductive whorl consisting of stamens.
  • Gynoecium: Female reproductive structure consisting of fused carpels.

Stamens

  • Stamens are the male reproductive organs.
  • Each stamen consists of anther and filament.
  • Anthers produce pollen.
  • Tomato stamens form a characteristic cone/cylinder around the female reproductive structure.

Gynoecium

  • The carpels are fused.
  • The ovary contains ovules.
  • The style connects the ovary with the stigma.
  • The stigma receives pollen.
  • After fertilization, ovules develop into seeds and the ovary develops into the fruit.

🐝 2.6 Pollination & Fertilization — Basic Plant Description

Tomato is predominantly self-pollinated. The position and structure of the stamens and stigma favour self-pollination, although environmental conditions and flower morphology can influence the efficiency of pollen release and fruit set.

  • Pollen is produced in the anthers.
  • Pollen must reach the receptive stigma for successful fertilization.
  • After pollen germination, the pollen tube grows toward the ovule.
  • Successful fertilization initiates seed and fruit development.
  • Temperature, humidity and flower condition influence pollen viability and fruit set.
📌 Important: Pollination, fertilization and fruit set are connected processes but are not identical. Successful pollination is followed by fertilization and subsequent fruit development.

🍅 2.7 Fruit

Botanically, tomato is a fleshy berry. The fruit develops from the ovary after successful fertilization. Its internal structure consists of the pericarp, locules, placenta and seeds.

Fruit Structure

  • Pericarp: Fleshy fruit wall.
  • Locules: Internal chambers containing seeds.
  • Placenta: Tissue to which the ovules/seeds are attached.
  • Seeds: Develop from ovules after fertilization.
  • Skin: Outer protective layer of the fruit.

Tomato cultivars may differ in the number of locules. Fruit morphology is therefore an important varietal and breeding character.

Tomato fruit cross-section showing internal locules and seeds.

🎯 PG/JRF Point: Tomato fruit is botanically a berry. The fruit develops from the ovary, while the seeds develop from ovules.

🌱 2.8 Seed

Tomato seeds develop from ovules following fertilization. Mature seeds are enclosed within the locules of the fleshy fruit and are surrounded by a gelatinous material.

Importance of Seed

  • Seed is the principal reproductive unit used for commercial propagation.
  • Seed quality directly influences germination and seedling establishment.
  • Healthy and genetically pure seed is important for uniform crop production.
  • Seed treatment and proper nursery management are important before transplanting.
🧠 Remember the sequence: Flower → Pollination → Fertilization → Ovule becomes Seed → Ovary develops into Fruit.
